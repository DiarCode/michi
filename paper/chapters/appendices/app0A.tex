\chapter{Appendix A: Dataset and Implementation Details}

\section{Astana Bus Network Topology}

The synthetic bus network was generated from OpenStreetMap data for Astana, Kazakhstan. The OSM extract was parsed to identify bus stops, which were then deduplicated using an 80-metre minimum separation threshold. Stops were assigned to one of four administrative districts (Esil, Almaty, Saryarka, Baikonur) based on bounding-box containment. Ten routes were defined with distinct corridor orientations (north-south, east-west, central, peripheral, express, north-loop, south-cross, university, residential, business). Each route was populated with up to 20 stops selected by spatial filtering and sorted by trajectory direction.

\section{Kazakh Holidays and Special Events}

The following holidays were treated as non-working days with reduced ridership:
\begin{itemize}
    \item January 1--2 (New Year), January 7 (Orthodox Christmas)
    \item March 8 (International Women's Day), March 22--23 (Nauryz)
    \item May 1 (Spring/Labor Day), May 7, May 9 (Victory Day)
    \item June 10 (Capital City Day), July 6 (Astana Day)
    \item August 30 (Constitution Day), October 25 (Republic Day)
    \item December 16--17 (Independence Day)
\end{itemize}

Special events included Astana Arena football matches, Khan Shatyr concerts, Expo exhibitions, Independence Day parades, Nauryz festivals, university graduations, Capital City Day celebrations, and Barys hockey games. Events were injected into the simulation with attendance-driven demand multipliers ranging from 5,000 to 60,000 attendees.

\section{Weather Simulation}

Hourly weather was drawn from a set of eight templates (clear, cloudy, rain, snow, blizzard, fog, extreme cold, heatwave) with seasonally adjusted sampling probabilities: clear ($p = 0.42$ in summer, $p = 0.18$ in winter), cloudy ($p = 0.30$ year-round), rain ($p = 0.10$ in summer, $p = 0.04$ in winter), snow ($p = 0.02$ in summer, $p = 0.28$ in winter), blizzard ($p = 0.0$ in summer, $p = 0.06$ in winter), fog ($p = 0.04$ in autumn, $p = 0.02$ otherwise), extreme cold ($p = 0.0$ in summer, $p = 0.05$ in winter), heatwave ($p = 0.0$ in winter, $p = 0.03$ in summer). Probabilities are renormalized to sum to one within each season. Temperature ranged from $-40$\textdegree C to $+45$\textdegree C, precipitation from 0 to 30 mm, and wind speed from 0 to 60 km/h. A 5\% probability of sudden weather change was included to simulate meteorological volatility.

The functional forms of the demand modulation weights (Eq.~\ref{eq:demand_modulation}) are:
\begin{align}
w_{\text{temp}}(T) &= \begin{cases} 0.55 + 0.0225 \cdot (T + 40) & T < -20^\circ\text{C} \\ 1.0 & -20 \leq T \leq 30^\circ\text{C} \\ 1.0 - 0.020 \cdot (T - 30) & T > 30^\circ\text{C} \end{cases} \\
w_{\text{precip}}(P) &= \exp(-0.08 \cdot P) \\
w_{\text{holiday}}(h) &\sim \text{Uniform}(0.30, 0.50) \\
w_{\text{event}}(e) &= 1.0 + \frac{a_e}{6{,}000}, \quad a_e \in [5{,}000, 60{,}000]
\end{align}
where $a_e$ is the attendance drawn for event $e$ and $w_{\text{event}}$ is clipped at $11\times$ to bound extreme event effects.

\section{LACMTA Open Dataset Reference}

The Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority (LACMTA) open dataset was accessed via the LACMTA Developer portal (\url{https://developer.metro.net/}); the specific product used is the "Daily Boardings by Stop" GTFS-derived feed covering January--June 2024, version 2024.06. After the train/validation/test split (70\%/15\%/15\% time-based, with the test set held out as the last 15\% of the chronological range to prevent temporal leakage), the preprocessed benchmark contains 62{,}304 hourly observation windows across 180 stations and 9 routes. Preprocessing scripts and the exact stop--route mapping used in the evaluation are documented in Appendix~B.

\section{Random Seeds and Reproducibility}

The three independent training runs reported throughout the paper use random seeds $42$, $123$, and $7$ (PyTorch and NumPy seeded; CUDA non-determinism is documented as a known source of cross-run variability of order $10^{-4}$ on R$^2$). All hyperparameter search trials use a separate seed schedule (seeds $11, 22, 33, 44, 55$). Baseline re-evaluations reported in Table~\ref{Tab.main_results} use the seed set $\{42, 123, 7\}$ for the proposed model; classical baselines (HA, Seasonal Naive, MA) are deterministic and therefore reported without variance; the unavailability of multi-seed re-runs for the neural and GNN baselines within the project's compute budget is acknowledged as a limitation in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_limitations}.

\section{Negative Binomial Likelihood}

The probability mass function for the Negative Binomial distribution is:
\begin{equation}
    P(y \mid \mu, \kappa) = \frac{\Gamma(y + \kappa)}{\Gamma(\kappa) \, y!} \left( \frac{\kappa}{\kappa + \mu} \right)^{\kappa} \left( \frac{\mu}{\kappa + \mu} \right)^{y},
\end{equation}
where $\mu > 0$ is the mean, $\kappa > 0$ is the dispersion parameter, and $y \in \mathbb{N}_0$ is the observed count. As $\kappa \to \infty$, the distribution converges to Poisson with mean $\mu$. The learned $\kappa = 7.2$ indicates moderate overdispersion (variance $= \mu + \mu^2 / \kappa$).
