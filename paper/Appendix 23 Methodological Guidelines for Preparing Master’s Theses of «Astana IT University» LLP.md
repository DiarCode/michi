## **1. General Provisions** 

1. The Methodological Guidelines for Preparing Master’s Theses and Projects at “Astana IT University” LLP (hereinafter – the Guidelines) define the requirements for the formulation of the topic, structure, content, and formatting of master’s theses and projects at “Astana IT University” LLP (hereinafter – AITU). 

2. The master’s thesis represents the final stage of the research work within the scientific and pedagogical master’s program, while the master’s project constitutes the final stage of the experimental research work within the professional master’s program. Their formatting shall be carried out in accordance with the established standards. 

3. The master’s thesis is a scientific research work. Based on the research conducted by the student, it develops new theoretical concepts, the combination of which may be qualified as solving a scientific problem of significant importance; alternatively, it presents scientifically grounded technical, economic, or technological solutions, the implementation of which contributes substantially to the development of the relevant field of science. 

4. The main results of the master’s thesis must be presented in at least one publication and/or one presentation at an international scientific or practical conference related to the thesis topic. 

5. The purpose of writing a master’s thesis is to: 

 demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the field of study, as well as the ability to synthesize or apply ideas within a research context; 

 apply knowledge, understanding, and skills to solve problems in new or interdisciplinary contexts; 

 use the acquired knowledge to address specific applied and scientific problems; 

 search for and interpret information to form judgments based on social, ethical, and scientific considerations; 

 effectively communicate ideas, conclusions, problems, and solutions to both specialists and non-specialists; 

 demonstrate the ability to independently conduct research, solve current problems, and present research results through publications and conference presentations. 

6. The master’s project is aimed at integrating the knowledge and skills acquired during the educational program and focuses on applying theory to practice and creating a complete, implementable product. 

7. The purpose of writing a master’s project is to: 

 identify a project idea based on scientific works (theoretical, research, or

## **2. Topic of the Master’s Thesis / Project** 

9. The topic of the master’s thesis or project is developed based on market needs. In doing so, real problems of industry, business, education, and science are taken into account. The topic must be relevant, correspond to the current state and development prospects of science, and align with the profile of the educational program. 

10. The topics of master’s thesis/projects and their scientific supervisors are assigned to students based on written applications indicating the topic in three languages and signed by the proposed supervisor. 

11. If there is a need to adjust the topic or change the scientific supervisor, the issue shall be considered at the meeting of the Department of Educational Programs. 

12. The topic of the master’s thesis or project may be revised upon the recommendation of the Department of Educational Programs by the decision of the Academic Council before the beginning of the second academic year. 

13. The scientific topic should consist of 10–12 words and be formed from three components: Scientific aspect + Title or direction + Area of application or stakeholders. Examples are provided in the table below:

An algorithm or a system cannot be considered a scientific result, as they represent the practical component of the work. 

14. The student formulates the purpose and objectives that need to be accomplished to achieve the stated goal.The description of the purpose should answer the following questions: 

- What result will be achieved or what product will be created? 

- By what means or methods will it be achieved? 

- Who or what will benefit from the result? 

It is not acceptable to formulate the purpose of the project using phrases such as “to study…” or “to research…”, as these words indicate the means of achieving the goal rather than the goal itself. 

15. The object of the research refers to the processes or phenomena that give rise to a problem situation on the basis of which the research is conducted and that have been selected for this particular study.For example: “the processes of implementing information technologies into an enterprise management system...”The subject of the research lies within the boundaries of the object. It may include processes such as: 

- development and creation of an information product; 

- design of project databases; 

- software development; 

- application of agile project management methods; 

- project time planning, risk and quality management; 

- building an effective project team. 

16. The research methods include the techniques and approaches chosen by the student for searching, collecting, analyzing, and interpreting data necessary to solve the identified problem.The student briefly describes what was studied and by which method, to confirm the logical consistency and validity of the chosen methodological approach. 

17. Data collection methods are divided into primary and secondary. In primary research, the student collects data directly using methods such as archival research, surveys, questionnaires, and interviews. In secondary research, the student uses existing, collected, and processed information. In both cases, it is important to identify the main sources of the necessary data.

## **4. Writing the Master’s Thesis / Project** 

22. The master’s thesis/project may be completed using materials obtained from industrial enterprises and companies, research institutions, as well as other commercial and non-commercial organizations. 

23. It is recommended that students meet with their supervisor at least once a week to discuss the progress of their master’s thesis/project and, if necessary, adjust their activities. 

24. Supervision of master’s students shall be carried out by a faculty member holding an academic degree of Candidate of Sciences, Doctor of Sciences, Doctor of Philosophy (PhD), or Doctor of Profile, corresponding to the relevant field of study, with at least three years of scientific and pedagogical experience. The supervisor must be the author of at least five scientific articles published within the last five years in journals included in the List of Scientific Publications Recommended for Publishing the Main Results of Scientific Activity approved by the authorized body

29. The following requirements must be met when writing the thesis/project: 

 Sources (references) must include academic works (books, textbooks, thesis, journal articles, and conference proceedings) and popular sources (nonacademic magazines, online articles, white papers). ## **5. Structure and Content of the Master’s Thesis / Project** 

30. The defense of the master’s thesis/project includes the preparation, formatting, and defense procedure. It is recommended that the master’s thesis consist of **50–60 pages** , and the master’s project consist of **40–50 pages** , excluding the list of references and appendices. 

31. Regardless of the research problem and chosen methodology, the structure of the master’s thesis/project must include the following sections: 

- 1) Cover Page 

- 2) Title Page 

- 3) Abstract 

- 4) Table of Contents 

- 5) Introduction 

- 6) Main Part 

- 7) Conclusion 

- 8) List of References 

- 9) Appendices (if necessary)

## **6. Formatting of the Master’s Thesis/Project** 

41. The master’s thesis/project must be prepared in the LaTeX editor using the approved template in accordance with GOST 7.32–2017 (Interstate Standard. Information, Library and Publishing System. Research Report) and the TeX language. 

42. Figures, tables, charts, and text must not extend beyond the margins defined in the approved template. 

43. Mathematical formulas, symbols, lists, and tables must be typed using TeX syntax. The use of graphic analogs imported as images or other graphical objects is not permitted. 

44. All files containing figures, diagrams, and charts must be stored in a separately organized folder in vector format (e.g., .eps). 

45. The use of Internet libraries is allowed. However, the student is responsible for ensuring that all libraries used in the project (stored in a separate folder) compile correctly into the final file. 

46. The project file of the master’s thesis/project, compiled in the LaTeX editor, must be submitted to the Department of Educational Programs together with the supervisor’s feedback. 

47. The master’s thesis/project is considered accepted only if it correctly compiles into a .pdf file on the computer of the staff member responsible for receiving the works. 

48. The electronic version of the master’s thesis/project is stored in the university’s cloud system (Moodle).
